Riftbound Meta

The Riftbound meta is constantly evolving, and shaped by new set releases, balance shifts, and player experimentation. As powerful cards and archetypes emerge, players adapt their deck choices to either take advantage of dominant strategies or counter them. Understanding these shifts is key to analysing why some archetypes remain relevant while others fade from prominence.

A Meta divided by Colour

Lying beneath legend diversity...

At first glance, Riftbound's early competitive landscape appears remarkably healthy, with a broad spread of legends represented across tournaments and ranked play. However, beneath this promising veneer of legend diversity lies a metagame heavily shaped by a single colour archetype: Body Rune (Orange) decks. While no individual legend has emerged as overwhelmingly dominant, Orange decks have become essential in meta play, with 38% of decks running Orange. But why is this the case?

The Rising Demand of Dazzling Aurora

Dazzling Aurora
9 Energy 2 Power · Gear
Dazzling Aurora has been a deck archetype since Origins; Riftbound's first ever set, offering a unique and powerful effect where you can play expensive units ignoring its costs. In theory, the card is designed with a natural counterbalance, costing 9 energy and 2 power, taking considerable time and resources before it can be played. Although it may pose great threat once active, it is not overwhelmingly difficult to beat, and can be effectively answered with the correct play.

The Power Inequality of Aurora paired with Elder Dragon

For just 9 energy and 2 power, Aurora enables a player to get a costly unit like Elder Dragon, which would normally require far more investment into play reliably and repeatedly. In a format where power ceilings are high and games end quickly, cheating massive high cost units makes it increasingly difficult to win the game against such players using this deck archetype.
